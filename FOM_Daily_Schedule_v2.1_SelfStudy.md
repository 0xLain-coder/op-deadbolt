## 4. HELPER GUIDE

> **For the person helping him study.** You are not the teacher and not the timekeeper. Your job is to keep the *system* honest so that he can keep the *mathematics* honest.

### 4.1 The role in one sentence

**Be the checker, not the solver.** His biggest stated challenge is independent problem-solving — he can follow a solution but struggles to find one. Every time a solution is handed over early, that muscle shrinks. Your most valuable move is a well-timed question.

### 4.2 Who owns what

| He owns | You own |
|---------|---------|
| Doing every block; writing the Milestone Card; logging errors | Reading the card and the evidence page; asking the one question |
| Choosing the day's mode (Green / Yellow / Red) | Never arguing with a Yellow or Red call — you only ask what changed |
| Solving, proving, writing | Holding the stuck-ladder timings; never solving |
| Keeping the recovery blocks | Protecting them from "just ten more minutes" |
| Proposing a dial change | Allowing **one** dial per week, with a reason in the changelog |

### 4.3 Daily — the 5-minute check-in (inside the shutdown, 20:45 – 20:55)

The shutdown comes straight after FOM-4, so the day's mistakes are fresh on the table — the best possible moment for a check-in.

| Step | Time | What to do |
|-----:|-----:|------------|
| 1 | 1 min | Look at today's **Milestone Card**: **HIT** or **MISS** for each block, FOM-4 included. Glance at the evidence page — no marking, just *"is there a page?"* |
| 2 | 1 min | Look at the **FOM-4 tally** at the bottom of the error log: **mistakes found / mistakes corrected**. Found but not corrected? Those items go to the Doubt Log — not onto tomorrow's plate (no doubling up) |
| 3 | 1 min | Read the **five scores** (Section 23.2). Two scores of 2 or lower → tomorrow is Yellow |
| 4 | 1 min | Ask exactly one question: **"What was the hardest ten minutes today?"** Listen; do not fix |
| 5 | 1 min | Read tomorrow's Milestone Card. Is it checkable, observable, binary? If not, ask him to rewrite it. Confirm tomorrow's mode. Leave — the free evening starts |

He ticks the **REV** row himself at 23:40; you see it at the next check-in. If you cannot be there at 20:45, he photographs the card and the log and sends them (Section 27.5).

If a day was missed or ugly, say nothing about hours. Ask what he needs, and apply the cut order (Section 24.1).

### 4.4 Weekly — Sunday sync (15 minutes inside the 45-minute Weekly Review)

1. Open the **evidence folder** (Section 25.11): count the week's HIT / MISS cards.
2. Read the **Energy Dashboard** (Section 23.3) together. Zone decides the next week.
3. Look at the **error log**: the most frequent code this week is the week's one improvement.
4. Choose **one dial** (or none). Write it in the changelog.
5. Check the **calendar of dates** (Section 4.9) — anything within 21 days gets a line in the plan.

### 4.5 The three-level hint ladder (what you may say, and when)

| Minute stuck (Tier 2) | Your move | What you must **not** do |
|----------------------:|-----------|--------------------------|
| 0 – 20 | Nothing. He is on the stuck ladder (Section 9.6) | Hover, sigh, peek at the page |
| 20 | **Level 1:** the *topic tag only* — "this is a divisibility-and-parity problem" | Name the key idea or the first step |
| 30 | **Level 2:** one *question* — "what happens for n = 1, 2, 3?" or "what stays the same each time?" | Write a line of the solution |
| 40+ | **Level 3:** the first line of the official solution, then close it | Show the whole solution before he has re-solved cold |

Tier 1 compresses these to 10 / 15 / 20 minutes; Tier 3 stretches them to 30 / 40 / 50. After any Level 3, he **re-solves cold the next day** and marks the problem **H**.

### 4.6 How to check a proof without doing it

Use his 10-point checklist (Section 9.7), but ask rather than tell:

- *"Which line is the weakest?"* (He must find it.)
- *"Where did you use the hypothesis?"* — a proof that never uses a given fact has a hole.
- *"What if n = 0? What if the set is empty?"* — edge cases.
- *"Read it like an examiner who wants to take a mark off. Where would they?"*

### 4.7 What the helper never does

1. **Adds hours** on top of the plan — if more is wanted, use a dial (Section 27).
2. **Skips recovery** for study — never Nap 1, the workout, the evening reading or lights-out.
3. **Compares** him with other students or with last month's hours.
4. **Reveals a solution** before the ladder has run.
5. **Treats a Yellow or Red day as a failure** — it is the system working.
6. **Does the work** — drawing the diagram, completing the algebra, writing the proof.

You are a supportive older presence, not a counsellor. Bringing in a parent or professional early is part of the job.

### 5.3 The rhythm of a week and of a month

- **Mon–Tue: build.** New material, Tier 1 → Tier 2; every evening FOM-4 closes the day's mistakes.
- **Wed–Thu: deepen and prove.** Tier 2 → Tier 3, written proofs, board-style writing in Physics.
- **Fri: integrate.** Spaced retrieval and mixed sets — nothing new; FOM-4 looks at the *whole week's* error pattern.
- **Sat: milestone.** The checkable test of the week's learning and a timed set — then a long Free Zone.
- **Sun: review and reset.** Analysis, recall without notes, English for pleasure, the Weekly Review, and next week's plan.
- **Every night:** the day's subjects are revised once more in the last block, then sleep.
- **Weeks 1–3 of every four:** full schedule. **Week 4 (and every 4th week): deload** — scheduled *before* you need it (Section 22.8). With the load this high, this is the single most important burnout-prevention move in the file.

### 5.4 Flex rules — how the timetable bends without breaking

You asked for a block-based timetable rather than a rigid clock. The clock times in Sections 6–8 are the **default layout**. These rules say how far it may bend.

**Rule 1 — Anchors don't move.** Wake-up and Nap 1 bend ±15 minutes at most; **dinner bends ±5; lights-out never moves later** — earlier is fine on Yellow, Red and Lite days (Section 3.1).

**Rule 2 — The slide rule.** Any study block may start up to **30 minutes earlier or later**, provided (a) the *order* of the blocks stays the same, (b) no anchor moves, and (c) it never runs into dinner or lights-out.

**Rule 3 — The swap table.**

| Swap | Allowed? | Condition |
|------|----------|-----------|
| PHY ↔ CHE | ✅ Yes | Any time — same load; the Break-2 mini-nap stays between them |
| C+E halves (CS ↔ ENG order) | ✅ Yes | Any time |
| FOM-1 ↔ FOM-2 | ⚠️ Rarely | Only when the day's new idea needs a longer practice run |
| PHY or CHE ↔ FOM-2 | ❌ No | A flat morning means Yellow mode (Section 22.3), not a swap |
| FOM-3 ↔ anything | ❌ No | It is anchored to your evening peak, after the workout and bath |
| **FOM-4** | ❌ Never | It needs the day's *finished* work, so it never moves before FOM-3 — and it never trades places with the free evening |
| **REV** | ❌ Never | It is the last block **by design**; it never moves earlier |
| Workout | ⚠️ Rarely | It stays before FOM-3 |

**Rule 4 — The overrun rule.** A block that is not finished at its end time *stops*. Write "unfinished: ___" on the Milestone Card and move on. An overrun never eats dinner, revision or sleep.

**Rule 5 — Done means the Milestone, not the clock.** The clock is a ceiling. If the day's Milestone is genuinely achieved with 30 minutes or more left in FOM-3, FOM-3 may stop (Section 9.10). **FOM-4 always runs.**

**Rule 6 — The energy map.** Use it to decide what to shrink first when energy is uneven.

| Block | Cognitive load | If energy is low… |
|-------|---------------|-------------------|
| FOM-1, FOM-2, FOM-3 | 🔴 Deep | Protect these; shrink Tier targets, not the blocks |
| **FOM-4** | 🟠 Deep-lite (structured, analytic) | Shrink to 30 minutes (X problems and the weakest proof only) — never skip |
| PHY | 🟠 Medium-deep | Shorten Part B (problems) before Part A (concepts) |
| CHE | 🟠 Medium | Swap with PHY or convert to flashcards + one numerical |
| C+E | 🟡 Medium, active | First to shrink — English half first |
| **REV** | 🟢 Light-medium (retrieval + reading, paper only) | Keep REV-M ≥ 25 minutes; shrink REV-A to 15 |
| Weekend S1–S4 | 🔴 / 🟠 Deep-to-medium | Skip the timed set before the milestone attempt |

### 6.3 Why the order is what it is

1. **FOM first, after a slow morning.** The window after sleep is when you feel refreshed. The 30-minute slow start (water, mobility, plan the day) means FOM-1 starts warm, not groggy.
2. **Breakfast after two deep blocks.** Three hours of FOM-1 and FOM-2 on an empty stomach works for many people; if it doesn't for you, take a few nuts or a fruit at Break 1 (Section 14.2).
3. **PHY and CHE are separated by a 30-minute break with a mini-nap, not stacked.** Two similar-load blocks back to back is where focus quietly collapses.
4. **Lunch, then Nap 1, in that order.** The nap sits inside the post-lunch dip. Thirty minutes is the ceiling — longer and you wake groggy.
5. **C+E follows the nap.** Code and writing are active; they pull you out of the post-lunch dip.
6. **The workout is the hinge.** It splits the day into two halves and resets attention before the second deep block. At 17:10 it leaves more than six hours before lights-out.
7. **FOM-3 is in the evening on purpose.** It is your own peak — right after a workout and a bath — and it is where the hardest work (proofs, Tier 3) belongs.
8. **FOM-4 comes after FOM-3, not before.** Analysis needs *finished* work to analyse. It is also a different gear: solving looks forward, analysis looks back — mixing them makes both worse.
9. **The shutdown sits between the analysis and the free evening.** The helper check-in lands right after you have just seen your day's mistakes — the best moment for "What was the hardest ten minutes?".
10. **Reading before dinner, revision after it.** The reading survives the tight night; the revision lands in the last position, where retrieval and sleep-based consolidation work best.
11. **REV is paper only.** Screens go off at 21:35; the last block uses cards, notebooks and book pages in dim light.

## 9. FOM BLOCK PLAYBOOK

### 9.1 Four blocks, four jobs

| Block | When | Length | Job | What you walk away with |
|-------|------|-------:|-----|-------------------------|
| **FOM-1 · Learn** | 07:30 | 90 min | Understand and rebuild material | Notes in your own words + 1–2 proofs reproduced from a blank page |
| **FOM-2 · Practice** | 09:15 | 90 min | Apply it under pressure | A problem set, every problem marked C / H / X |
| **FOM-3 · Prove & Lock** | 18:05 | 90 min | Write it properly: the day's key proof and the hardest problem | One clean written proof + a Tier 2/3 attempt + a Milestone check |
| **FOM-4 · Analysis** | 19:45 | 60 min | **Entirely analysis:** review your work → find *all* your mistakes → correct them | A coded error log for the day, a corrected version of every flagged item, a tally |

The fixed internal clock of each block (it never changes — only the *content* changes by day):

| Block | Sub-steps (minutes) |
|-------|---------------------|
| FOM-1 | Set-up 5 → Part A 40 → micro-break 5 → Part B 35 → close-out 5 |
| FOM-2 | Pick problems 5 → Set A 40 → micro-break 5 → Set B 35 → **mark C / H / X 5** (marking only — FOM-4 does the logging and diagnosis) |
| FOM-3 | Reset 5 → Part A 35 → micro-break 5 → Part B 35 → Milestone check 10 |
| FOM-4 | Set-up 3 → **Review 20 → Identify 15 → Correct 20** → close 2 |

### 9.2 FOM-1 rules (Learn)

1. **Pen and blank paper are always on the desk.** The book is closed more often than it is open.
2. **Predict before you read.** Cover the next line of a derivation and guess it. Right or wrong, your brain now cares about the answer.
3. **Try to prove before you read the proof.** Give any theorem five honest minutes first. Reading a proof you have already struggled with teaches ten times more.
4. **No highlighting.** Rewrite each idea in your own words: *statement → why it's true → one example → one non-example.*
5. **One idea per page.** When you reconstruct it later, you will know exactly where to look.
6. **Read the Common Errors box as a warning about *you*.** Assume you are about to make that mistake.
7. **End every block with a "Still fuzzy:" line.** Naming the gap is half of closing it.
8. **Videos and articles:** not before you have made an honest attempt (Section 24.4).

### 9.3 FOM-2 rules (Practice)

1. **Pick the set before the timer starts.** Choosing problems is part of the 5-minute set-up, not something done mid-block.
2. **Use the tier ladder** (9.5) so you are practising at the right difficulty, not the comfortable one.
3. **Use the stuck ladder** (9.6). Struggle on purpose — but with a leash.
4. **Mark every problem C / H / X** the moment you finish it.
5. **Mark, don't diagnose.** The diagnosis happens in FOM-4, with a cooler head and the whole day's work in front of you. After the ladder has run, mark the problem and move on.
6. **No phone, even for "just checking a formula".** If you need a fact, use your notes.

### 9.4 FOM-3 and FOM-4 rules

**FOM-3 · Prove & Lock (18:05)**

1. **Write for an examiner.** Every claim has a reason. Use the 10-point checklist (9.7) *while* writing; the full audit happens in FOM-4.
2. **Part A (35):** the day's key result as a proof. **Part B (35):** the day's hardest problem (Tier 2 → 3), with the stuck ladder.
3. **Finish with the Milestone check.** Tick or untick *honestly* — no partial credit, no "nearly".
4. **Evening mind, evening work.** This block is where Tier 3 and proof-writing live; do not spend it on reading.
5. **If the day's Milestone is hit early, FOM-3 may stop** — see the early-stop rule in 9.10.

**FOM-4 · Analysis (19:45)** — *your rule: entirely analysis.*

1. **Nothing but analysis.** No new problems, no new reading, no planning, no shutdown tasks.
2. **Three stages, in order:** Review → Identify → Correct (full protocol: 9.13).
3. **"All" means all.** Not only the **X** and **H** problems — also a **C**-marked solution with a weak step, a proof with an unjustified line, a note with a wrong statement, and a lucky guess.
4. **Every flagged item gets a code, a one-line cause and a corrected version** written by you.
5. **Correct cold first:** try the fix without the book; check the source only afterwards.
6. **What you can't correct in the block goes to the Doubt Log** (Section 25.7) — never onto tomorrow's plate.
7. **Close with the tally:** flagged __ · corrected __ · to Doubt Log __.
8. **Zero flagged mistakes is a warning light, not a trophy.** Re-run the 10-point checklist on your weakest proof before you close.

### 9.7 The 10-point proof checklist

Before you call anything a proof, check:

1. ☐ **Given vs. to-prove** are both written out.
2. ☐ **Notation** is defined before it is used.
3. ☐ **Method announced** (direct, contradiction, induction, cases, construction…).
4. ☐ **Every step has a reason** — a theorem, a definition, or an earlier line.
5. ☐ **Cases are exhaustive** and (where it matters) disjoint.
6. ☐ **Quantifiers are right** ("for all" vs "there exists").
7. ☐ **Edge cases** are handled: 0, 1, negatives, the empty set, equality.
8. ☐ **No pattern-spotting dressed as proof.** Three cases checked is not a proof.
9. ☐ **The conclusion matches the claim** — word for word, not approximately.
10. ☐ **Grader test:** read it as a sceptical examiner. Could they find a gap? If yes, fix it.

### 9.13 The FOM-4 analysis protocol — step by step

**What it is.** One hour, every weekday, 19:45–20:45, spent *entirely* on your own work from today — the FOM-1 pages, the FOM-2 sets, the FOM-3 write-up. **What it is not:** new problems, new reading, planning, or the shutdown.

**Set-up (3 min).** Lay out today's FOM-1 pages, the marked FOM-2 sets, the FOM-3 write-up, the Milestone Card, the error-log notebook, a pen in a *different colour*, and the 10-point checklist (9.7).

**Step 1 — REVIEW (20 min).** Read your work like a sceptical examiner.

- Re-read **every line** of every proof and solution — not only the ones you marked **H** or **X**.
- Underline anything you cannot justify in one phrase: an unjustified step, a hidden assumption, a missing case, unclear notation, arithmetic you did not double-check.
- Hunt for **right answer, wrong reason** and **lucky guesses** — a **C**-marked problem can still hide a flaw.
- Check the FOM-1 pages for statements of definitions and theorems that are wrong or incomplete.
- Put a number in the margin beside each flag. **Don't fix anything yet.**

**Step 2 — IDENTIFY (15 min).** For each numbered flag, write one line in the error log: date · where (FOM-1 / 2 / 3, which problem) · **code** (C / T / A / P / M / S / D) · **cause** in one line. "Cause" means *why you made the mistake*, not what the right answer is. Count the flags. This list is the day's complete mistakes list.

**Step 3 — CORRECT (20 min).** Work through the flags in this priority order:

1. **Concept gaps (C):** rebuild the idea on a blank page.
2. **Proof gaps (P):** rewrite the proof so the gap is closed, line by line.
3. **Unsolved problems (X):** re-solve cold. If you are stuck after 8 minutes, use Hint 1 and then Hint 2 of the ladder — no more.
4. **Slips and misreads (A / M / S / T):** redo the line or the problem correctly.

Check against the source only *after* your own correction. Write the corrected version under the flag's number. If time runs out, finish in priority order and send the rest to the Doubt Log.

**Close (2 min).** Write the tally at the bottom of the day's error-log page: **flagged __ · corrected __ · to Doubt Log __.** Set a re-solve date 3–7 days ahead for the hardest item, and make a retrieval card for any lesson worth keeping.

**The weekly pattern**

| Day | FOM-4 focus |
|-----|-------------|
| Mon – Wed | The day's own work |
| Thu | The day's work **plus** a spot-check of Monday's proofs for hidden gaps |
| Fri | **Week-level:** count the week's codes, find the most frequent one, choose the **one improvement** for next week |

**What "good" looks like.** After a month, flagged items per day trend *down*, and the share that are proof gaps (**P**) rather than slips (**A**) shifts towards the more interesting kind. You are making better mistakes.

**Overflow rule.** If you finish early, stop. Finishing early is not a licence to open new problems.

### 13.2 Nap 1 — the standard nap (14:45–15:15)

2. **Thirty-minute slot:** lie down at 14:45, **alarm at 25 minutes**, up and drinking water by 15:15.

### 22.5 Sick-day protocol

If you have a fever, vomiting, or genuinely feel unwell:

1. **Skip study.** Tell a parent or guardian; see a doctor if it is more than a mild cold.
2. Water, light food, sleep. Nap 1 becomes real rest. Lights-out as early as you like.
3. **Return gradually:** the first day back is a Yellow day, the second is Green if you feel fine.
4. A day or two of illness costs far less than a week of dragging yourself through blocks.

| Travel and waiting | Maths retrieval cards **or** English audio/reading — **no new material**, nothing that makes you travel-sick | up to 30 |
| At college | Attend; use idle gaps for a 15-minute Maths queue pass | 15 |
| On return | Water, a proper snack; **Nap 1** (≤ 30 min) if home before 17:00 | — |
| Evening | **FOM-2 (short)** | 60 |
| Evening | **FOM-4 (short)** — flag, code and correct today's few problems | 30 |
| Fixed | Shutdown → free evening → reading → **dinner 21:55** | — |
| Last block | **REV (short):** Maths 25 + others 15 | 40 |
| Optional | **PHY or CHE (mini)** — only if energy is Green | 40 |

**Study on a college day: about 3 h 55 m** (4 h 35 m with the optional mini-block). Dropped: the other of PHY / CHE, C+E, FOM-3, the workout (a 20-minute easy walk instead). **Lights-out 23:45 at the latest — earlier if the travel was long.** **The morning after:** if sleep was under 7 hours, it is a Yellow day.

### 22.7 Exam fortnight mode (college exams, practicals, boards)

Starts **14 days before the first paper** and lasts until the last one. *Ask the college for its exam, practical and journal calendar now.*

| Block | Exam-fortnight use |
|-------|--------------------|
| FOM-1 | 60 min: Maths queue + **one** problem. FOM stays alive, nothing more |
| FOM-2 | 60–90 min: past papers or practice for the **next exam** |
| FOM-3 | 60 min: formula and method consolidation for the next exam |
| FOM-4 | 60 min, unchanged in shape: review → find all mistakes → correct — now aimed at *the day's exam practice* |
| PHY / CHE | Both slots go to the subject with the next paper; the other keeps 40 min of revision |
| C+E | The next CS or English paper, if any; otherwise English reading for pleasure |
| REV | The last block stays, tied to the exam subjects |
| Weekend | Exam-paper practice instead of the FOM timed set; keep both Free Zones |
| Sleep and recovery | **Unchanged.** Exams punish tired brains more than ill-prepared ones |

New FOM modules pause during the fortnight; restart the Monday after the last paper.

### 22.8 Deload week (every 4th week — Weeks 4, 8, 12, 16 …)

Deload weeks are **scheduled before you need them**. With a Standard week at 62 hours, this is the most important burnout-prevention move in the file.

| Block | Normal week | Deload week |
|-------|-------------|-------------|
| FOM-1 and FOM-2 (Mon–Fri) | 90 min each | 90 min each (keep) |
| FOM-3 (Mon–Fri) | 90 min | **Free** — outdoors, a hobby, extra rest |
| FOM-4 (Mon–Fri) | 60 min | 30 min — light analysis of FOM-1 and FOM-2 only |
| PHY and CHE (Mon–Fri) | 80 min each | 50 min each |
| C+E (Mon–Fri) | 80 min | 40 min — one half only: English reading for pleasure *or* one CS task, no output |
| REV (Mon–Fri) | 70 min | 40 min (Maths 25 + others 15) — still the last block |
| Workout | 35 min | Keep, easier |
| Saturday | S1 + S2 + PCM check + C+E + REV-W | S1 as a **low-stakes** attempt (90 min) + a 30-minute combined PCM check; no timed set, no REV-W |
| Sunday | S3 + S4 + calm literacy + review + REV-W | S4 (45 min) + a 30-minute light review; everything else free |
| Sleep | Fixed block | **Unchanged** — lights-out may move earlier on three nights |
| Extra | — | **One outing or activity you genuinely enjoy** (not study-related) |

A deload week holds about **35 h 45 m** of study (58% of a Standard week) and **21 h 50 m** of FOM including Maths revision (60%). You lose little progress and gain the energy to keep the other weeks going. *Weeks 4, 8 and 12 fall on 30 Nov, 28 Dec and 25 Jan (Section 26.2).*

### 22.9 Festival / family-event day

1. Keep the **five anchors** (Section 3.1) as far as you can: wake-up, Nap 1, workout (or a walk), dinner, lights-out.
2. Run the Yellow-day study load — FOM-1 + FOM-2 only, plus the short FOM-4 and REV if you are home by dinner.
3. Don't double up the day before or after. Being present at the event is part of a sustainable life.

## 23. BURNOUT EARLY-WARNING SYSTEM

### 23.1 A hard week vs burnout

| A hard week | Burnout |
|-------------|---------|
| Tired, but a good night or a free Sunday restores you | Tired even after rest; the tiredness has been there for weeks |
| You're stressed but still curious | Curiosity has gone flat; dread when opening a book |
| Performance dips, then recovers | Performance keeps falling despite more effort |
| You want a break | You feel numb, cynical or detached from goals you used to care about |

This table is for orientation, not diagnosis. If you aren't sure which column you're in, treat it as the right-hand column and use the ladder in 23.5.

### 23.2 The daily five scores (30 seconds, in the shutdown)

Rate each 1 (bad) to 5 (great): **Sleep quality · Energy · Mood · Focus · Motivation.** Also write the three sleepiness scores (Section 13.4). Template: Section 25.4.

### 23.3 The weekly Energy Dashboard (Sunday, 18:45)

Average all 35 scores (5 scores × 7 days).

| Weekly average | Status | Action next week |
|----------------|--------|------------------|
| ≥ 3.5 | 🟢 Green | Normal week — keep the Full load (27.2) |
| 2.5 – 3.4 | 🟡 Yellow | Step down to **Lite** (27.2); REV-A and C+E optional; one extra free evening; recheck on Wednesday |
| < 2.5 | 🔴 Red | Start a deload week now and use the response ladder (23.5) |

Three overrides:
- **Any single score averaging ≤ 2.0** for the week → the week is at least Yellow, whatever the overall average says.
- **Any day with three or more scores of 2 or lower** → the next day is Yellow.
- **Average sleepiness ≥ 3.5 on five or more days** → add sleep (lights-out 22:15) *before* touching any block (13.4).

### 23.4 Weekly warning-sign checklist

Tick any that were true on three or more days this week:

**Body**
- ☐ Waking exhausted despite 7+ hours in bed
- ☐ Frequent headaches or stomach aches
- ☐ Big changes in appetite
- ☐ Getting ill more often than usual

**Mind**
- ☐ Re-reading the same page without absorbing it
- ☐ Dread when you sit down to study
- ☐ Racing thoughts at night
- ☐ Blanking on things you knew last week

**Feelings**
- ☐ Irritable or snapping at people
- ☐ Feeling flat, numb or "nothing matters"
- ☐ Anxiety spikes before study blocks or tests
- ☐ Feeling hopeless about your goals

**Behaviour**
- ☐ Procrastinating through blocks
- ☐ Avoiding friends and family
- ☐ Skipping recovery rules (nap, sleep, workout, free evening)
- ☐ Scrolling to avoid studying

| Ticks | Reading |
|------:|---------|
| 0–3 | Normal. Keep going |
| 4–7 | Yellow. Follow Level 1–2 below |
| 8 or more | Red. Follow Level 2–3 below |
| "Hopeless" ticked | Go straight to Level 3 — today |

### 23.5 The response ladder

| Level | Trigger | What to do |
|------:|---------|-----------|
| **1** | Yellow for 3 days | Yellow-day protocol (22.3), or step down to **Lite** (27.2). Turn the heaviest evening into rest. Lights-out 30 minutes earlier |
| **2** | Yellow for a week, or one Red day | Start a **deload week now**. Drop FOM-3 and C+E. Extra outing. Talk to one person about how you're feeling |
| **3** | Two Red days in two weeks, 8+ ticks, or low mood or anxiety that lasts | **Talk to a parent or guardian and a teacher or mentor you trust. Consider seeing a doctor or counsellor.** Do not wait until after the exam |
| **4** | You feel hopeless, trapped, or think about hurting yourself | **This is not a scheduling problem.** Tell a trusted adult right now. In India you can call **Tele-MANAS** — free, 24×7, in many Indian languages: **14416** or **1-800-891-4416**. If you are in immediate danger, call your local emergency number |

### 23.6 Things that feel like discipline but are really burnout

- Studying through a fever or a migraine.
- Cutting sleep "just this week".
- Skipping Nap 1, the workout or the free evening "to fit in one more block".
- Feeling guilty about the Free Zone or the free evening.
- Comparing your hours with someone who posts their hours online.
- Treating a Yellow day as a personal failure.

### 23.7 Things that actually protect you

- Deload weeks **scheduled in advance**.
- The free evening and the paper-book reading, **every night**.
- Two Free Zones a week that nobody gets to cancel.
- Telling someone early.
- Sleep that is boring and consistent.

## 24. RULES THAT PROTECT THE SCHEDULE

### 24.1 The cut order — when a Green day is running behind

*(This is for a Green day that is slipping. A whole-day switch to Yellow or Red uses the templates in Section 22 instead.)*

| Order | What to cut | Why |
|------:|-------------|-----|
| 1 | **REV-A** shrinks 30 → 15 (Physics 5 · Chemistry 5 · CS / English 5) | Keeps REV-M and keeps REV the last block |
| 2 | **C+E** shrinks 80 → 60 — the English half goes 40 → 20 | Lowest cognitive cost; the weekend holds a catch-up |
| 3 | **FOM-4** shrinks 60 → 30 — today's **X** problems and the weakest proof only | Analysis is compressed, never skipped |
| 4 | **PHY and CHE** Part B (problems) shrinks — 80 → 60 min each | Concepts survive; problem volume drops |
| 5 | **FOM-2** Tier targets drop (fewer problems, same ladder) | Same discipline, less volume |
| **Never** | Night sleep (lights-out 23:45) · Nap 1 (minimum 15 min) · the mini-nap (it may become a rest) · meals and dinner at 21:55 · the bath · the workout (it becomes a 20-minute walk) · FOM-1 · FOM-3 · REV-M (minimum 25) · the free evening · the evening reading | These are what make the other blocks work |

### 24.2 Never double up

Missed work is **never** added on top of tomorrow. It goes to the weekend, or it is dropped. Doubling up is how one bad day becomes a bad week.

### 24.3 The FOM floor

On any day you are not ill, FOM gets **at least 20 minutes** — even if it is one Tier 1 problem on a Red day. It keeps the thread alive and removes the "I've fallen off" feeling.

### 24.4 Phone and video rules

1. **Phone in another room** during FOM-1, FOM-2, FOM-3, FOM-4, PHY, CHE and REV — and it stays outside until the night routine.
2. **No phone for the first 30 minutes** after waking.
3. **No phone in bed.** Use a physical alarm clock.
4. Notifications off; games and social apps removed or blocked during study hours.
5. If you need a fact mid-block, **write the question down** and look it up at the next break.
6. **Videos and web articles** (you use them sometimes — good): chosen *before* the block, **≤ 15 minutes per block**, at most 1.25× speed, pause and predict the next step, notes written *after*. In FOM-1, only **after** an honest attempt at the proof. Never as a way to avoid a stuck problem before minute 20 of the ladder.
7. For English speaking practice, the phone is a **voice recorder only**, on Do Not Disturb.

### 24.5 The desk-reset ritual (2 min, before every study block)

Clear the desk → water bottle in place → blank paper and two pens → timer visible → notes closed unless the block says otherwise → phone away.

### 24.6 People and promises

- **Tell your family the sacred times:** Nap 1, the workout, dinner (21:55) and lights-out (23:45).
- A small "Do not disturb — back at __:__" card on the door works.
- Your **helper** is your accountability buddy: ten minutes at the shutdown, forty-five on Sunday — "What did I commit to? What did I do?"

### 24.7 The comparison rule

Your yardstick is **your Milestones**, not anyone else's hours. Hours measure sitting; Milestones measure learning.

### 24.8 The change rule

- **One dial per week.** Two weeks to judge it. No changes during exam fortnights except as Section 22.7 says.
- Every change gets a line in the changelog at the bottom of the file, with the reason.

## 28. FAQ & TROUBLESHOOTING

**Does FOM reach my 6–7 hours a day?**
Yes, in the sense that counts: 330 minutes of FOM blocks (Learn, Practice, Prove & Lock, Analysis) plus 40 minutes of Maths revision is **6 h 10 m** every weekday, and about 36 hours across the week. That is more than twice the pace the FOM book assumes (26.3).

**Why isn't the shutdown inside FOM-4 any more?**
Because you asked for FOM-4 to be *entirely* analysis. The shutdown (card, log, worry dump, helper check-in) now sits in its own 10-minute block straight after it — which also puts the helper check-in right after you have seen the day's mistakes.

**What actually happens in FOM-4?**
Three things, in order: **review** your own work like an examiner (20 min), **identify** every mistake with a code and a cause (15 min), **correct** each one yourself (20 min). Nothing else. The full protocol is Section 9.13.

**What does "book reading" mean in the last block?**
It is the way the non-Maths subjects are revised at night: you read the day's own pages and notes — Physics 10, Chemistry 10, CS 5, English 5 — then close the book and recall three key points. Maths gets the first 40 minutes as retrieval from cards and blank pages. (If you meant a separate paper book at bedtime, see "Open decisions" below.)

**I hit the Milestone early. Now what?**
FOM-3 may stop (the early-stop rule, 9.10) — don't open a new problem; let the time become a longer break or a calmer free evening. **FOM-4 still runs**: a ticked Milestone does not mean a flawless day.

**FOM-4 found nothing. Did it fail?**
A day with zero flags is a warning light, not a trophy. Re-run the 10-point checklist on your weakest proof; there is almost always a step that "felt obvious" and was never justified.

**The last block makes me wired and I can't fall asleep.**
Check three things: (1) is REV staying on retrieval and book reading, with *no new problems*? (2) is dinner light? (3) are screens really off from 21:35? If sleep onset still takes more than 30 minutes on three nights, put the 10-minute walk back at 21:45 (13.5) and shrink REV-A to 15.

**I can't focus after lunch.**
That is the planned dip. Check lunch size, keep Nap 1 to 25 minutes of lying down, and start C+E standing for 10 minutes (13.4). Don't try to push a deep block through it.

**I feel guilty about the Free Zone / free evening.**
They are a training load, not a reward. Your brain consolidates learning when you rest; skipping them gives you the *feeling* of progress and the opposite result.

**I'm behind on the module calendar.**
Step 1: check the Energy Dashboard. Green → shrink milestones. Yellow → step down to Lite or deload. Only then move the window (26.4).

**Physics feels impossible.**
Go back to the audit idea: rebuild one chapter at a time, derive rather than memorise, and use P1 problems until 8 of 10 are clean (10.2, 10.4). The weekly check will tell you whether it is working.

**I can't solve anything alone.**
That is the skill you are training. Use the stuck ladder to the letter, mark honestly (**C / H / X**), re-solve cold the next day, and watch the **H** share fall over a month. FOM-4 is where each failure becomes a correction.

**Can I swap Saturday and Sunday?**
Only as a whole-week move (27.1), and never so that Milestone Day stops sitting directly before Review Day.

**I want to study at night.**
Your evening is already full of study you chose: FOM-3 (18:05), FOM-4 (19:45) and the last block (22:30–23:40). Anything after 23:45 costs the sleep that makes tomorrow's 07:30 FOM-1 work — and the sleep block is fixed.

**What if I sleep through Nap 1's alarm?**
Two alarms (phone + a physical clock, both across the room). If it happens twice a week, shorten the nap to 20 minutes and check your night sleep.

**My family's dinner isn't at 21:55.**
Dinner is a fixed 35-minute window. If your family eats earlier, eat with them and let the free evening and reading absorb the time; REV still starts at 22:30 — digestion first. If dinner can't finish before 22:35, trim the evening reading, never the sleep or REV-M.

**Can the free evening be YouTube?**
Yes — it is yours. Keep it non-study and stop at 21:35. If it is eating your sleep, shrink it rather than the sleep.

**What if I'm sick or travelling?**
Sick: Section 22.5. Travelling: a Yellow day with the five anchors held as far as possible; no doubling up afterwards (24.2).

**How will I know it's working?**
Four signals: the milestone hit-rate (≥ 60%, then 70%); the top error code *and* the flagged-per-day count in FOM-4 falling month on month; the Energy Dashboard staying mostly Green; and the baseline re-test at Week 13 beating the setup-day snapshot.

**What if my helper and I disagree about a dial?**
Run both for two weeks, one at a time, and look at the Energy Dashboard and the hit-rate. The numbers decide.
